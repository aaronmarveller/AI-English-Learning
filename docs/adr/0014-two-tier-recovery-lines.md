# A `needs_retry` Turn speaks a two-tier Recovery, and the pool it used to speak becomes `steerLines`

Each Conversation Goal carried a flat three-line `needsRetryLines` pool, and a `needs_retry` Turn spoke exactly one line from it — the first Goal in canonical order the Goal Report marked `failed`, or the Focus Goal's when nothing was `failed` (issue #49). One pool had to cover two different situations: a learner who made a recognisable attempt that did not come through, and a learner who attempted nothing at all. The Goal Report already tells them apart — `failed` is the first, everything `untouched` is the second, and ADR-0006's collapse of off-topic into `needs_retry` is what makes that pair sufficient — so a `needs_retry` Turn now speaks a **two-tier Recovery** instead of one flat line (the old pool itself survives the change, renamed — Decision 5). Tier 1 is a **nudge followed by the Goal's question**: the shared "Sorry, I didn't quite get that." followed by that Goal's question when an open Goal's Goal Report is `failed` (the `unclear` variant), or that Goal's off-topic nudge followed by its question when every open Goal is `untouched` (the `off-topic` variant). Tier 2 — from the learner's second consecutive `needs_retry` Turn on the same Focus Goal — is a single **direct example**, `You can say "Hi" or "Hello."` for `greeting`. Its four lines are word-for-word identical in tickets 8 and 10, so one line per Goal serves both variants. Which Goal's Recovery is spoken is unchanged from issue #49; which tier it gets is the learner's **Retry Streak**, how many `needs_retry` Turns in a row the current Focus Goal has had. Nothing crosses the Judge/Submit seam for any of it — the Judge, the wire protocol and the Verdict derivation are untouched — and the tier is chosen entirely client-side from the Goal Report the Judge already returns.

**Decision 1 — the first tier never reveals an Accepted Response; the second tier may.** Section 3 of `docs/ai-configuration.md` said `needs_retry` lines never reveal an Accepted Response, and Section 1's Global Constraints made that a standing rule: "Never reveal an Accepted Response, even while encouraging a retry." Tickets 8 and 10 both ask for the opposite on the second try — their second-tier columns hand the learner an example (`You can say "I'm good" or "I'm okay."`) — because that is what progressive support is: the first try gives another chance without giving the answer, and the second gives the answer's shape. The rule becomes tier-scoped, and the Global Constraint is qualified rather than dropped. First-tier Recovery lines never reveal an Accepted Response; second-tier lines may. The silence reminder stays on the safe side of the line by construction, because it is always first-tier wording (Decision 3).

**Decision 2 — the first tier is a sequence, not a combined line.** Ticket 10's table spells its Check-in cell out as "Let's keep going. How are you today?", and the tempting implementation is one authored line per table cell. Rejected for the same reason ADR-0013 decision 2 rejected ticket 4/6's combined utterance: keying one half of a reply to the other *is* composition, spread across two pools, and a combined line needs a recording of its own where the nudge and the question can each have one. The first tier is therefore the nudge line followed by the Goal's question line, both spoken in full, and the steer pools stay the single source of the question wording. Two asymmetries in the resulting table are consequences of that, not oversights: `checkin` has no authored question at all, because its question is the Check-in pool's own "How are you today?" ("Hi! How are you today?" and "How's it going?" are its siblings), reused rather than written a second time; and `response` and `closing` have a question but no off-topic nudge, because the tables author none for them, so there the question is the whole of the off-topic variant. One question per Goal is shared by both variants, which is why ticket 10's shorter off-topic wording for `greeting` ("What would you say?") is not a second question: the variant picks the nudge, and the question is the Goal's.

**Decision 3 — the silence reminder is the same sequence.** Ticket 9's example reads "Take your time. How are you today?", and it is the silence-nudge pool's line followed by the Goal's Recovery question — the same two-line sequence rather than a second set of authored lines, for Decision 2's reason again. That is also what keeps Ticket 9 inside Decision 1 without a rule of its own: the reminder is only ever first-tier wording, so it never reveals an Accepted Response.

**Decision 4 — the tier comes from a Retry Streak, not from `retryCounts`.** The streak counts consecutive `needs_retry` Turns against the *current* Focus Goal: tier 1 on the first, tier 2 from the second. It resets on any `accepted` Turn — including one that leaves the Focus Goal open, because that is progress — and a new Focus Goal starts a fresh streak. `support_requested` Turns never touch it — the client resolves Chinese input before the Judge, so the streak counts attempts only, and a Chinese Turn is not one. `retryCounts` cannot serve as the tier's counter: it deliberately never resets and counts every `needs_retry` Turn ever judged against a Goal, because what it is for is `passedFirstTry` and Review's "needed support for <Goal>". A Goal that was retried once and then left open while a later Goal was achieved has a non-zero `retryCounts` entry while the learner's most recent Turn was progress, so reading the tier off it would hand that learner the direct example as if they had just failed again. The two counters answer different questions — `retryCounts` is per-Goal history and never resets, the streak is the current run on the Goal being asked about and resets on progress — so both are kept: `retryCounts` with its meaning unchanged, the streak as a persisted field of its own.

**Decision 5 — `needsRetryLines` becomes `steerLines`, and only `greeting` and `response` keep theirs.** The pool stops being what a `needs_retry` Turn speaks — tier 1's lines are the Recovery's, tier 2's are the direct examples — which leaves it exactly one job, and it is not a retry's: the line Emily speaks *unprompted* toward an open Goal after an `accepted` Turn, which is the borrowed steer for `greeting` and `response`, neither of which has a steer pool of its own (issue #50's rule, unchanged). The field becomes optional and only those two Goals carry it: six lines, unchanged, with the name and the job description moving to match the job, and the audio following the name, because a manifest id is the audio filename — `needs-retry-<goal>-<n>` becomes `steer-<goal>-<n>`. `checkin`'s and `closing`'s three lines each are deleted, and their recordings with them, because nothing could ever speak them: `checkin` steers from the Check-in pool and `closing` from the Closing pool, so #56's Recovery had replaced the only caller those pools ever had — a pool no code path can draw from is dead content, and its audio files would be orphans outside the manifest. A pool still called `needsRetryLines` while no `needs_retry` Turn speaks from it is a name that lies about what its entries are for, which is the drift `CONTEXT.md`'s glossary exists to prevent.

**Decision 6 — the silence reminder replaces the bare silence nudge.** The pool used to send one bare line ("Take your time!") after a while. Emily now speaks Decision 3's reminder instead, under the rules Ticket 9 asks for, all of which the implementation enforces: the window is 15–20 s of the learner's own silence, measured after Emily's line and only once the Handoff Gap has ended, so the timer does not run while Emily is speaking, during the Handoff Gap, or while the microphone is open (`CONTEXT.md`'s Turn-Taking, whose floor rules ADR-0008, ADR-0010 and ADR-0011 settle; the microphone-listening snapshot the timer reads comes from `src/lib/speech-synthesis.ts`). The reminder never changes Goal Progress and never records a Turn — it is appended as a support message, like the Chinese-input nudge — and it never repeats the same nudge line twice in a row. It is not a `needs_retry` Turn and it does not advance the Retry Streak: silence is not a failed attempt, and the streak counts attempts.

## Considered options

- **Keep one flat pool per Goal and just prepend a nudge to it.** Rejected: it cannot tell an unclear attempt from an off-topic reply, so one of the two always gets the wrong register — a learner who said something garbled is told to keep going, or a learner who attempted nothing is told their meaning did not come through. The Goal Report already carries the distinction (`failed` versus everything `untouched`), so forking on it costs no new signal and no model change.
- **Author every first-tier line as one combined string** — twelve of them, one per Goal per situation (unclear, off-topic and silent), each a nudge and a question in a single line. Rejected for ADR-0013 decision 2's reason — a composed line needs its own recording and recomposes wording the pools already own — and because it duplicates the question text: the same questions would exist both inside the combined lines and in the pools the sequence draws from, with no single source that says which is right. The sequence keeps the steer pools the one place that wording lives.
- **Derive the tier from the existing `retryCounts` instead of a new streak counter.** Rejected: `retryCounts` deliberately never resets and counts *all* `needs_retry` Turns judged against a Goal, because it is what makes a later accepted Goal not-first-try and what Section 5's Suggestion reads. A Goal that was retried once and then left open while a later Goal was achieved would carry a non-zero count although the learner's last Turn was progress, so the learner would jump straight to the direct-example tier, which is not a second try at anything. The tier needs a counter that resets, and `retryCounts` must not be one.
- **An escalating silence reminder that reaches tier 2 as well.** Out of scope: Ticket 9 asks for one reminder and never to give the answer immediately, and the streak is about attempts — silence is not one. A learner who goes quiet twice hears the same reminder shape again, with the no-repeat rule keeping the nudge itself varied.

## Consequences

- Nothing crosses the Judge/Submit seam: `src/lib/practice-turn-protocol.ts` and `src/lib/practice-judge.ts` are untouched, and `deriveVerdict`'s all-or-nothing rule still reads only the Goal Report. Which Goal's Recovery is spoken still comes from `selectRecoveryGoal` (first `failed` Goal in canonical order, else the Focus Goal). The `failed` half of the Verdict is now one predicate both callers read (`src/lib/goal-progress.ts`'s `reportHasFailedGoal`), so the Verdict and the Recovery's `unclear`/`off-topic` variant cannot come to disagree about the same report.
- Goal Progress, the Focus Goal and the all-or-nothing rule are untouched too: a Recovery Turn saves nothing and never moves the Focus Goal, and the next `accepted` Turn returns the learner to the normal flow.
- `src/content/lesson.ts` carries the Recovery content (`RecoveryScript`: the shared `unclear` nudge, the two off-topic nudges, one question per Goal, one direct example per Goal) beside the renamed `steerLines` pool — now an optional field that only `greeting` and `response` carry, six lines, unchanged — while `checkin`'s and `closing`'s pools are deleted, because their steer comes from the Check-in and Closing pools and no code path could ever speak those six lines.
- `src/lib/emily-reply-selector.ts`: `selectEmilyLinesForTurn`'s `needs_retry` branch picks the Goal by the unchanged rule and then the tier from a new `focusRetryStreak` input; `selectSilenceReminder` now returns the reminder — the nudge line followed by the Goal's question — rather than one pool line.
- `src/lib/practice-state.ts` persists the streak (`retryStreak`) alongside the per-Goal `retryCounts`, which keeps its meaning: it counts `needs_retry` Turns per Goal, never resets, and still feeds `passedFirstTry`, which is what Section 5's Suggestion reads — never the streak. A `needs_retry` Turn bumps `retryCounts` and the streak; an `accepted` Turn resets the streak and leaves `retryCounts` alone.
- `src/components/practice/practice-page-content.tsx` gates the silence timer: it starts only once the floor is the learner's — Emily's line finished, the Handoff Gap over, the microphone closed — and it reads that from `src/lib/speech-synthesis.ts`'s Turn-Taking and microphone-listening snapshots instead of arming on the message alone.
- Audio: ten new English texts (the shared `unclear` nudge, the two off-topic nudges, the three authored Goal questions, and the four direct examples) need pre-generated files under `recovery-*` ids. The Check-in question and the silence-nudge lines already have files, so the reused halves of those sequences add nothing. The six renamed steer lines keep their texts, and so their recordings' content, but a manifest id is the audio filename (`public/audio/<id>.mp3`), so those six files are renamed to `steer-<goal>-<n>.mp3` for `greeting` and `response` before the runtime's exact-text lookup finds them again. `checkin`'s and `closing`'s steer files are deleted rather than renamed: their lines are gone, and the manifest's own integrity test fails on an MP3 no entry claims.
- `docs/ai-configuration.md` Section 1's `needs_retry` and reveal rules and Section 3's `needs_retry` and silence subsections are rewritten for the two tiers, the rename and the reminder; `CONTEXT.md` gains Recovery, Retry Streak and Silence Reminder.
