# The `response` Goal means asking Emily back, and Emily answers an ask-back when she gets one

ADR-0004 aligned `response` with the team's "AI Configuration" doc by accepting a **single short phrase** — and its whitelist was `"Thank you."`, `"Thanks."`, `"How about you?"`, `"And you?"`. v2 tickets 2/3/4/6 read the same Goal differently: `response` is *asking Emily back*. Ticket 3's table maps "I'm good, thanks." to a Check-in answer, ticket 4's table is headed "Ask-back Pre-generated Responses", and ticket 12 treats a thank-you as an optional politeness marker to reward in feedback rather than a Goal to credit. The two readings conflict on one input — a bare "Thanks." — and on nothing else, so this is a refinement of ADR-0004 rather than a rejection of its rule. We are settling on the tickets' reading: **`response` is achieved by asking Emily a question back, and never by thanking her.** A bare thank-you leaves the Goal `untouched`, exactly as any other message that attempted no Goal does (#49's boundary) — it is not `failed`, because a thank-you is not a garbled ask-back. ADR-0004 is superseded.

Two more decisions came with the same set of tickets, both recorded here because they are the same change seen from three angles.

**Emily keeps her two-line sequence; only the pool wording changes.** Ticket 4/6's Emily lines fold her answer and the closing steer into one utterance ("I'm good too, thanks! Have a nice day!"). ADR-0012 already rejected composed lines for multi-Goal Turns, and every composed line would need its own recording. So the combined sentences are *not* authored as verbatim lines: their halves become the pool wording, and Emily speaks the answer from the asked-back Response pool followed by a Closing-pool line — the same reaction-then-steer sequence she already speaks. The pool therefore loses the "thanks for asking" acknowledgements (there is no check-in to acknowledge when the learner asked back in an earlier Turn) and gains the ticket's own answers.

**The reaction is due whenever the learner asked back, not only when the check-in landed in the same Turn.** This is the one behaviour change the tickets ask for, and it is what makes ticket 4's own example work: Emily asks how the learner is, the learner answers ("I'm good." → "That's good!", Emily waits), and only on the *next* Turn does the learner ask "How about you?". The check-in was achieved a Turn earlier, so a reaction keyed to "checkin achieved in this Turn" would leave Emily silently steering to Closing and never answering the question put to her. The reaction rule becomes: **Emily answers when the learner asked her back, and otherwise acknowledges when the check-in was achieved in this Turn** — the asked-back sub-pool in the first case, the did-not-ask-back one in the second. The complementary half is the wait ticket 3 asks for: a check-in acknowledged *without* an ask-back gets exactly one line and no steer toward `response`, because `response` is a question the learner has to decide to ask.

**The opening line collapses to the ticket's single line.** Ticket 2 fixes Emily's opening to "Hi! I'm Emily. It's nice to meet you."; #46 had declared the 5-line opening pool out of scope, leaving the choice to this PR. We take the single line: the pool existed to vary a bare "Hi!"/"Hello!", and a self-introduction has nothing to vary without inventing four more of them. The Lesson keeps the pool's *shape* (an array, picked by `pickRandomOpeningLine`), so a future Lesson can widen it again.

## Considered options

- **Keep ADR-0004's reading and treat an ask-back as one way to achieve `response`.** Rejected: it leaves "Thanks." and "How about you?" in one Goal's whitelist while ticket 12's feedback library calls a thank-you a politeness marker, not an achievement — and it would let a learner complete Practice without ever asking Emily a question, which is the conversation skill this Lesson exists to teach. Either a thank-you completes `response` or it does not; a whitelist that hedges is the one thing the Judge cannot be given.
- **One new combined line per learner-ask-back wording, verbatim from the tickets.** Rejected for the same reason ADR-0012 rejected composed lines: six combined lines, six new recordings, and a pool that has to grow again the moment a seventh ask-back phrasing is recognised.
- **Replace the `response` Goal with a "say thanks" Goal** so the whitelist and ticket 12's marker agree. Rejected: the Goal exists to practise keeping a conversation going, and asking the other person something back is what does that. A thank-you is optional politeness riding on the check-in answer, which is what ticket 3's table already shows.
- **Give `response` a steer pool of its own** (a line that invites the learner to ask something back, spent on the Turn after the acknowledgement). Deferred, not rejected: it needs new lines and new audio, and ADR-0012 already named a dedicated steer pool as the fix if the borrowed lines read wrong. Ticket 3 asks for the wait, and a wait is what the composition does.

## Consequences

- `response`'s Accepted Responses are the ask-back expressions from tickets 4 and 6 (`"How about you?"`, `"And you?"`, `"You?"`, `"What about you?"`, `"How are you?"`, `"How are you doing?"`, `"How about yourself?"`); `"Thank you."` and `"Thanks."` leave the whitelist, and its `learningGoal` says outright why. Its three `needs_retry` lines are re-authored to point at the question back instead of at an acknowledgement.
- The Check-in pool, both Response sub-pools, and the `checkin` Goal's Accepted Responses are re-authored from tickets 3/4/6's tables (docs/ai-configuration.md section 3). Changed lines get regenerated audio; unchanged ones keep their existing recordings.
- The Judge's `failed` definition loses the thank-you path (`src/content/practice.ts`, `src/lib/practice-judge.ts`): `response`'s attempt is a question back, and no other. `learner_asked_back` stops being "the boolean that only matters after a check-in" — it is the signal that the `response` Goal was achieved, and it now decides whether Emily answers at all.
- The composition in `src/lib/emily-reply-selector.ts` gains the asked-back reaction, and the `focusGoal === "response"` short-circuit fires whenever a reaction line was spoken rather than only when the check-in was.
- `matchedAcceptedResponse` (`src/lib/turn-record.ts`) flips for learners who answered with a thank-you: those Turns are `needs_retry` now, so no record is written at all, and a thank-you inside an otherwise-accepted Turn no longer matches `response`. Review's Conversation highlight quotes an ask-back expression rather than "Thank you." for the same reason.
- The judgment eval (scripts/eval-judgment.ts) gains ticket 2/3/4/6's cases — "I'm good, thanks." alone (check-in only), "How about you?" alone, "Hi! I'm good." (greeting + check-in, then Emily waits), and "Hi! I'm good, thanks. How are you?" (three Goals, answer and closing steer) — and its two cases that credited `response` for a bare thank-you are corrected.
- ADR-0004's "one short phrase completes the Response step" rule survives in the only form that still holds: **one question back is enough**, with no acknowledgment or added detail required.
